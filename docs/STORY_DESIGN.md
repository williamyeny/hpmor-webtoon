# Story Design — Arc One

This file runs the `STORYTELLING.md` method on the HPMOR opening (source chapters 1–10, plus the
start of 12) and records every decision. Later arcs should append their own run of the method
below, not overwrite this one.

---

## Step 1 — The seed, and the feeling to protect

**Seed:** An eleven-year-old raised by an Oxford scientist gets his Hogwarts letter and decides
the correct response to magic is *the scientific method*.

**The feeling:** *Glee with a shadow.* The fizzing delight of the cleverest kid in the room
running headfirst into a world that makes no sense — and, underneath, a slowly cooling unease
that the kid himself might be the most dangerous thing in it.

Everything below protects that pairing. If a page is only funny, it needs a shadow somewhere. If
a page is only dark, it needs a light somewhere.

## Step 2–3 — Premise

Candidates considered (abridged): the hero as fish-out-of-water comedy; the hero as detective of
magic; the hero as child genius nobody listens to; the hero as famous-for-a-miracle-he-can't-explain;
the hero as a Dark Lord in the making; McGonagall as the lead (the one adult who has to handle him);
Draco as the lead (the tempter who gets tempted).

**Chosen premise:** *A boy who has never once been taken seriously discovers he's famous for
defeating a Dark Lord — and the more he learns, the more everyone (a witch, a rival, and a
talking hat) suspects he could become the next one.*

Contradiction built in: the symbol of the Light has an icy streak that the story keeps catching
in the corner of its eye. It passes the stranger test ("wait — Harry Potter as a potential Dark
Lord?"), the engine test (his cleverness *creates* trouble in every scene), and the distance test
(it is recognisably not canon Harry Potter).

## Step 4 — The question underneath (theme)

**"If you're sure you're the smartest person in the room, who gets to tell you no?"**

Put more gently: *Is being clever enough to be good?* Harry's answer at the start is "Yes — and
I don't need anyone else, because every adult I've met is less sane than me." The honest case
for Harry: the adults around him really *are* often wrong, dismissive, incurious (Dad won't even
watch the owl test; McGonagall calls the pouch "magic" and stops thinking). The honest case
against: the moments where he's most certain he's right (the Neville prank, blackmailing
McGonagall, planning to guillotine blood-purists) are exactly the moments the Hat reads as
"Dark Lord material".

The arc does **not** settle the question. It answers a smaller one — *will he let anyone in?* —
with a cautious **yes, one person**: McGonagall. (See Step 10.)

## Step 5 — The promise

**Primary experience:** laughter. **Secondary:** wonder (the wizarding world, seen by someone who
takes it *seriously*) and a creeping unease.

**One-sentence promise:** *You close the arc grinning at the last joke, a little in love with
these characters, and slightly afraid of what Harry is going to turn into.*

**Must-have moments** (readers would feel robbed without them), and how each is delivered:

| Must-have | Delivery |
|---|---|
| The Letter | An owl-less letter on a table in a house drowning in books; the parents fight over it. |
| First proof of magic | Dad levitated among his own bookshelves, deadpan: "Huh." |
| "You turned into a cat!" | Full meltdown splash; physics itself literally going down the drain. |
| Diagon Alley reveal | Tall splash panel as the brick wall unfolds. |
| The wand choosing him | Sparks + the sense of magic "like an eye opening"; brother-wand chill. |
| Draco at Madam Malkin's | The flattery duel + Lucius + McGonagall in synchronised "WHAT?". |
| Platform 9¾ | He has to *believe* — the most un-Harry thing possible. |
| Meeting Hermione | Her POV. The 2-4-6 game. A rival who is actually his equal. |
| First sight of Hogwarts | (Added — HPMOR skips it.) Castle across the black lake. |
| The Sorting | Mindscape duel with the Hat. "SLYTHERIN! …Just kidding! RAVENCLAW!" |

## Step 6 — Harry

- **Wants (visible):** to *understand* magic — and, until he gets there, to get to Hogwarts and
  get his hands on everything. Tracked on-page by his notebook, his experiments, and the
  running gag of the pouch.
- **Wants (underneath):** to be *taken seriously*. Given a visible form: Harry's private
  **points tally** for adults ("Minerva McGonagall, +1 point").
- **Believes at the start:** Adults can't be trusted to get anything right; if you want something
  done sanely, do it yourself.
- **Why:** the pan-and-the-mugger memory (Mum laughed it off); a father who "Shows Interest" but
  never changes his mind; a lifetime of being the youngest person in every room.
- **Good at:** fast reasoning, Fermi estimates, quoting the right study, reading people *after the
  fact*, talking faster than anyone can object.
- **Flaw:** his strength over-extended: certainty. He can justify anything clever. The dark
  version is "the cold": when angry enough, his blood goes cold and he starts calculating people.
- **Would never:** hurt his family. (The abuse-question scene is where that line is visible.)
- **Early defining choice:** In a fight between his parents, he proposes an *experiment* — and when
  nobody will help, he goes out alone and yells at the sky for an owl, mortified, because "I will
  use the scientific method even if it makes me feel stupid."

Watchable because of: humour, skill, and sympathy (the kid nobody listens to).

## Step 7 — Pressure

- **The world's rules** (magic is inconsistent; wizards are incurious) frustrate his want.
- **Adults** (McGonagall, parents) hold absolute authority over him.
- **The Dark Lord** — "dead", then *not dead*. The prophecy he joked about turns out to exist.
- **His own cold side** — the real antagonist of this arc. Given faces: the frost motif; the Hat.
- **Quirrell** — seeded only (sense of doom), for future arcs.

## Step 8 — The cast as a system (arc one)

| Character | Wants | Answer to the theme question | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **McGonagall** | To keep children safe and the rules intact | "Rules and trust keep people safe; cleverness without them is dangerous." | The core relationship. The adult who *does* change her mind. |
| **Dad (Michael)** | To be a Good Parent; to stay right | "The smart person is the one who's already right." | What Harry could become: clever and closed. Loves him in books, not words. |
| **Mum (Petunia)** | Her family safe; her son not to become her sister | "Love first; arguments later." | Warmth. The fear of losing him to magic. |
| **Draco** | To make a powerful friend; to make Father proud | "The strong decide what's true." | Mirror/tempter. The boy whose father *does* take him seriously. Shows what cleverness serves when nobody questions it. |
| **Hermione** | To be tested; to be the best; to have a friend | "Being good *is* following the rules and being right." | The equal. Rival and future partner. Her POV lets us see Harry from outside ("scary"). |
| **Neville** | Not to be hurt | — | The victim of Harry's clever prank. Makes the Hat's accusation land. |
| **The Sorting Hat** | To Sort well (and, this once, not to be conscious) | "Your warmth needs other people." | The verdict. The arc's final opponent. |
| **Fred & George** | Chaos | "Rules are for fun-breaking." | Tempters in the fun direction. The Order of Chaos. |
| **Quirrell** | ??? | ??? | The shadow at the edge of the frame. |

Core relationship arc: **Harry ↔ McGonagall**, from *"Hopeless. Both of his parents were just
hopeless"* (every adult is hopeless) → a witch who laughs at his rules → a witch who cries with him
→ a witch who asks the question nobody asked (what happened to *you*?) → a witch he threatens →
a witch who *listens* and changes her actions (+1 point) → the adult he chooses to tell a secret to
at the end.

## Step 9 — World rules that matter this arc

- Underage magic is detectable and forbidden at home → Harry can't experiment until school.
- The Killing Curse cannot be survived — except once. Nobody knows why.
- Obliviation exists → *any* memory could be missing. (Harry's counter: a pre-arranged signal.)
- The Hat reads thoughts as they form; it can't reveal other children's secrets.
- Comed-Tea: something surprising happens once per can. (Reality bends for comedy.)
- The pouch returns only what *you* put in, by name.
- Wizards are incurious. Ten Muggle-raised students a year. Nobody has ever run an experiment.

## Step 10 — The ending of the arc

**Central question of the arc:** *Who is Harry Potter going to be?* (Hero, Dark Lord, or
something new.)

Endings considered: (1) Ravenclaw, triumphant; (2) Hufflepuff — he takes the Hat's advice;
(3) Slytherin for real; (4) Ravenclaw but he loses the Hat's argument and knows it; (5) the Hat's
prank — *Slytherin*, then Ravenclaw — so he lives through the horror of the wrong choice
for three seconds.

**Chosen:** (5), which is HPMOR's, *plus* a pulled-forward beat from chapter 12: in the seconds
after, Harry realises the Hat just did to him what he did to Neville — a frightening prank "for
his own good" — and feels it from the inside. His first act as a Ravenclaw is to put
"Apologise to Neville Longbottom" at the top of his list, and to decide to tell **McGonagall**
about the strange Slytherin whisper instead of chasing it alone. That is the arc's answer:
*he will let one adult in.* Cost: he chose the House that "strengthens the coldness", knowingly.

**Final image (mirror of the opening):** Episode 1 ends with Harry alone in a back garden,
holding a letter up to an empty sky and getting no answer. Episode 12 ends with Harry at a long
table, among other children, looking up at an enchanted ceiling full of stars — and at the edge
of the frame, the Defence Professor looking back at him.

## Step 12 — The opening

- **Hook:** a cold open lifted from HPMOR's chapter-one epigraph (moonlight, a silver thread,
  black robes falling, "someone screams a word"). It promises the comedy has teeth. It pays off in
  source chapter 114; see `LOOSE_ENDS.md`.
- **Before:** the Oxford house of books; Harry ignored mid-argument.
- **Mirror:** Harry under an empty sky.
- **Disrupt:** Mrs Figg takes the letter. "What."

## Step 13 — The ladder (arc one)

1. Prove magic exists (garden test) → *succeeds*, but now everything he knows is wrong.
2. Get to the wizarding world → *succeeds*, but he's famous for something he can't remember, and
   his parents died for him.
3. Get money / tools → *succeeds* by bargaining; he secretly pockets gold (a small crime —
   plants the trunk-shop payoff).
4. Handle people (Draco) → comic *triumph*, but McGonagall now thinks he's contagious chaos.
5. Understand magic (pouch experiments) → *fails* — the rules don't mean anything. "Claims the
   territory in the name of Science" anyway.
6. Get the healer's kit → the worst-case habit hurts a stranger; McGonagall asks what happened to
   *him*. **Midpoint turn:** the Dark Lord isn't dead. The comedy is now about something.
7. Force the truth out of McGonagall → wins by *blackmail* — his first cold victory, and it scares
   him. Then wins again the *right* way (trunk shop). +1 point.
8. Make an ally (Draco) → wins, but learns what Draco really is; *low point of his faith in this
   world* ("note to self: overthrow government").
9. Make a friend (Hermione) → wins (and loses the upper hand); she calls him "scary".
10. Get Sorted where he belongs → the Hat nearly takes it from him. **Climax.**

## Step 14 — Who knows what

- **Audience > Harry:** we see Dumbledore's reaction to McGonagall's report; Draco's letter to
  Lucius (and Snape's name); Quirrell's twitch at the head table; Snape crushing his goblet.
- **Audience < Harry:** the Hat conversation's full content (the Hall only sees minutes pass).
- **Chosen confusion:** the cold open; Harry's sense of doom around Quirrell; Draco's "Snape";
  "Salutations from Slytherin to Slytherin".

## Step 15 — Form

Vertical-scroll webtoon, ~40–60 tiles per episode, 12 episodes this arc. Each episode opens a
question and ends on a turn, a laugh-button, or a cliffhanger. See `EPISODES.md`.
