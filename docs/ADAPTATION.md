# Adaptation Log

Episode by episode: what changed from HPMOR and why. Source chapter numbers refer to `hpmor/chapters`.
Guiding rule (from the brief): *change only when the change is a marked improvement* — for a
wide audience, reading on a phone, in a visual medium.

General conversions applied everywhere:

- **Internal monologue → visible.** HPMOR lives in Harry's head. Webtoon readers skim; long
  thought-paragraphs become (a) short `inner` captions in Harry's voice, (b) pencil-note
  diagrams (Caveat handwriting) for his calculations, (c) visual metaphors (physics going down a
  drain; frost for the cold).
- **Lectures → one line + one picture.** Science references are kept when they're funny or
  characterful, cut when they're just citations. A rule of thumb: one science idea per scene, and
  it must *do* something to the scene.
- **Show the told.** Several beats HPMOR tells in summary are shown (Harry biting the maths
  teacher, Petunia's potion, Dad's bookshop raid, the Neville prank).
- **Emotional beats get space.** Silent panels, tall panels, and gutters are used where the prose
  lingers (the garden silence, the story of the Potters).

## Episode 1 — A Day of Very Low Probability (ch. 1)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **Cold open** built from the chapter-1 epigraph (moon, silver thread, falling robes, scream). | HPMOR's epigraph is an easy-to-miss promise that this comedy will turn deadly. A webtoon needs a hook in the first three panels; this one is both a hook and a far-future plant (ch. 114). Kept non-graphic: the scream is unreadable, the blood is a red wash. |
| "The wettest July anyone could remember." | Seasonal mood (the brief asks for rainy weather). HPMOR doesn't specify weather; July rain in Oxford is plausible and cosy. |
| Harry is on-panel from the first interior shot, peeking over a book. | In HPMOR he only speaks up halfway through the argument. Readers need to know whose story this is immediately. The "eyes over the book" panel gives him a silent, likeable introduction. |
| Petunia's backstory is shown as a **sepia memory**, including Vernon and "Dudley Dursley". | Long monologue → four pictures. The Dudley joke is kept because it's the funniest line in the chapter and a gift to Potter fans (and it quietly tells new readers "this is a different timeline"). |
| Dad's dismissal → Harry's hurt close-up → captions about respect → **a frost flicker** on "Sometimes Harry wanted to scream at his father." | Plants the visual language of Harry's "cold side" on page one, tied to its root (not being taken seriously). Pays off in Ep 7, 9, 10, 12. |
| Letter-writing montage with the letter itself legible; wax seal H.J.P.E.V. | Delight + character ("madness, with style"). |
| The silent-house beat split into Dad/Mum side-by-side panels. | The chapter's sharpest observation ("not arguing was worse") becomes a composition. |
| Garden: the shout is a **full-bleed low-angle panel**; Mrs Figg's cat stays on the fence and looks at the reader. | Big comic payoff needs size. The cat is a small plant for Figg-as-watcher (Dumbledore). |
| Ends on "What." after a tall silent panel under a single first star. | The star sets up the arc's final image (Ep 12: Harry under the enchanted ceiling). |

## Episode 2 — Everything I Believe Is False (ch. 2)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **Added: the silent dinner + "DING-DONG" + McGonagall on the doorstep, rain not touching her.** | HPMOR cuts straight to the demonstration. A must-have moment in any Potter story is *the stranger at the door*. The rain gives a wordless first proof of magic that Harry notices; her first line calls back the owl joke. |
| Levitation is a tall panel; Dad deadpan among his own bookshelves. | The chapter's image, made literal: the sceptic, hoisted by his own library. |
| "Huh." / "Huh." as a split panel. | Comic timing via layout. |
| The meltdown is split into a big splash + a visual metaphor spread (planets, apple, atoms, brain, equations swirling into a drain). | HPMOR's best paragraph ("And then a woman turned into a cat, so much for all that") needs a picture of what broke. |
| 26-hour sleep cycle shown as a **clock diagram**; McGonagall says "*in time*" and a tiny hourglass appears. | The Time-Turner plant (ch. 14) — visible to rereaders, invisible to first-timers. |
| **Shown: seven-year-old Harry biting the maths teacher.** | Told in HPMOR; far funnier seen. |
| Flaming zebras get their own panel. | Obviously. |
| **Added: Harry thanks McGonagall at the door "for *showing* us, instead of just telling us"; she softens; leaves as a cat.** | Starts the arc's core relationship on an explicit note: the thing Harry values is being *shown* evidence and being taken seriously. It also gives McGonagall a warm exit instead of ending on a gag. |
| Ends with Harry awake by candlelight: "Which means I'm going to need a *lot* more books." | Warm button + sets up Dad's book raid (Ep 8). |

## Episode 3 — The Boy Who Lived (ch. 3)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **Added:** a calendar page (every day of August crossed off) and Harry practising incantations with a pencil at 3 a.m. | Gives the month-long wait a beat, shows his impatience, and reminds the reader of the 26-hour sleep cycle. |
| Charing Cross Road with Muggles walking past the pub without looking. | A must-have image of the hidden world, missing from HPMOR's text. |
| Doris's handshake shown as a close-up of two hands and a falling tear. | The chapter's most moving beat; it makes fame *costly* before it's funny. |
| McGonagall's foot-stamp is a full-bleed "CRACK". | Comic power moment for her; establishes she is formidable. |
| **Quirrell: the "wrongness" effect** (colour-split bands + a low hum written as "mmmmmm"). | Converts "I had the strangest feeling I shouldn't shake his hand" into a visual motif that recurs every time Harry is near him (Ep 11, Ep 12). |
| The brick archway opens in three panels (wall → shuffling bricks → arch) then a tall reveal framed by the arch. | The Diagon Alley reveal is a must-have spectacle. |
| **The history of Voldemort as shadow theatre** (silhouettes on sick-green, blood-orange and ember backdrops); Yermy Wibble's death shown as a nailed newspaper, not a body. | Keeps the horror (it must land for Harry to cry) while staying suitable for a wide audience. Silhouettes also keep Lily & James "unknown" to Harry. |
| "Voldemort" gets the frost treatment. | Ties the name's coldness to Harry's own cold streak — a quiet rhyme for rereaders. |
| The note of confusion is a faint "?" alone in the margin. | Rereader payoff; first-time readers barely register it, which is exactly how HPMOR frames it. |

## Episode 4 — The Efficient Market Hypothesis (ch. 4 + ch. 5 first half)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **Added:** the banking hall and a mine-cart ride ("IS THIS A ROLLER COASTER?!"), with a dragon's eye in the dark. | HPMOR skips straight into the vault; the ride is a delight beat and a must-have Gringotts image. |
| Arbitrage and the Fermi estimate are pencil diagrams. | "Skill is fun to watch" — but only if you can see it. |
| McGonagall's "*You.*" is a red, backlit dread panel. | The comic peak of the chapter needs size. |
| The pocketed Galleons are revealed immediately (a close-up of a bulging pocket, "Not *quite* without any more trouble."). | HPMOR reveals it at the Moke shop; showing it at once lets the reader enjoy the heist *with* Harry. |
| The Moke-shop heist as a three-panel hand sequence. | Pure visual comedy. |
| Ends on Madam Malkin's plain shopfront. | Sets up the next episode's chaos by its very boringness. |

## Episode 5 — Six Minutes (ch. 5 second half)

Renamed from "The Fundamental Attribution Error" (that idea is now delivered in Ep 4), to a title that promises the comedy. Otherwise very close to HPMOR: the flattery duel, the rapid-fire "Shut up / autograph" exchange as a six-panel grid, Lucius as a backlit full-bleed entrance, the synchronised "WHAT?" and recoil, the four-panel "Madam Malkin looked back silently for four seconds", the wine drip. It's the funniest chapter; the job was to not step on it.

## Episode 7 — The Winner Shall Lose (ch. 6 second half + Aftermath)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **The wand moment gets its own sequence:** sparks, then an abstract "eye opening" panel. | HPMOR's line about magic being a sense he'd always had with his eyes closed is the most beautiful idea in the chapter; it deserves a picture. |
| Brother wand: Ollivander's line in frost; Harry's Bayes note. | The coincidence should *feel* cold. |
| Frodo/Mount Doom imagined as a silhouette panel. | Laugh + foreshadow. |
| **The blackmail scene is the first full "cold" sequence:** tinted blue, frosted, angular pale balloons in small caps; Harry's tactical options listed and crossed out in cold captions. | The arc's key visual motif, at full strength, at the story's midpoint. |
| **Harry's ledger: "Adults who changed their actions because of something I said — Minerva McGonagall, +1 (first entry)."** | Makes HPMOR's "+1 point" into the relationship's milestone. It is echoed in Ep 12. |
| The lip-biting close-up **cut** (it read as a blood spot on a mouth — unclear at phone size). | Clarity. |
| Aftermath: Dumbledore's office, three identical McGonagall panels (open mouth / close / open). | The gag works best as timing. |

## Episode 8 — Reciprocation (ch. 7 first half)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **Added: the Greatest Second-hand Bookshop Raid Ever** as a montage (Oxford, Cambridge, London, Bath) and the thirty boxes in the trunk's cavern. | HPMOR mentions it in one sentence at the station. Showing Dad's love *before* "Do you think I bought you enough books?" makes the goodbye land. It also answers Harry's accusation that Dad never takes him seriously: Dad dumps his schedule the moment Harry says it matters. |
| "Resonant doubt" at the barrier drawn as a loop diagram. | Makes the barrier a comic *thinking* problem. |
| Ron's scene condensed; **cut** Harry's "Don't see any reason for him to exist? — Pretty much." and replaced with Harry resolving to make it up to Ron. | The original line makes Harry needlessly cruel toward a sympathetic kid and costs reader goodwill without paying anything off. |
| Harry's "Mr Bronze" disguise: a red-and-gold striped scarf wound over his face; a sweatband over the scar in Muggle London. | A running visual gag and an instantly readable silhouette. |
| The Quibbler headline is a full-page panel, then a double spit-take. | Timing. |

## Episode 9 — The Moon (ch. 7 second half + Aftermath; the prank from ch. 8)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **Draco's threat against Luna changed** from sexual violence to "make her pay — curse her so St Mungo's can't fix it — then Obliviate her; Father has the votes." | Wide-audience suitability. The *meaning* is unchanged: a privileged child who is sure he's above the law, and whose enemies aren't people. |
| The platform goes silent (blurred, desaturated) when Draco casts his privacy charm. | Makes "when Draco reached inside his robes" legible. |
| The guillotine thought is **drawn** (list of names, a guillotine, in frost). | The Hat cites it in Ep 12; readers must remember it. |
| The Moon photograph is a full-bleed plate in an open book, followed by rocket plates. | The chapter's emotional centre. |
| **The Neville prank is shown live**, played as a romp, then a single panel asks: "Wasn't it?" | In HPMOR it's told after the fact. Showing it makes the Hat's accusation in Ep 12 hit the reader as hard as it hits Harry. |
| Aftermath kept: the ink-dripped "Dear Father", the letter, Lucius's reply naming Snape, the letter in the fire. | Mystery hook; Snape appears in Ep 11. |

## Episode 12 — Self Awareness (ch. 10; ch. 12 moved in)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **The Hat conversation is staged inside Harry's head**: an endless library that goes warm, void, or frozen with the argument; four House doors light up gold (Hufflepuff/Gryffindor) or ice-blue (Ravenclaw/Slytherin) when the Hat explains warmth vs. coldness. | A long telepathic argument becomes a place. |
| The Hat's evidence is shown as inset flashbacks (the guillotine; Neville's terror). | Pays off Ep 9 on the page. |
| "What happens if you fail?" as a staccato of five small dark panels, then an explosion. | Timing and pressure. |
| "You sly little bastard" → "You sly little devil." | Rating. |
| **Harry's frozen horror at "SLYTHERIN!" is paired with an inset of Neville's face from the platform.** | Makes the arc's lesson — he just felt what he did to Neville — visible without a word. |
| **Moved from ch. 12:** the Slytherin whisper; Harry's to-do list and "altering the distant past"; the decision to tell McGonagall and to apologise to Neville; the "+1" on his anti-Dark-Lord programme; Quirrell's "Salutations, my young apprentices" speech. | Gives the arc a resolution (a cautious *yes* to trusting one adult), a stinger (the whisper), and a hook for the next arc (Quirrell). |
| **Cut (for now):** Dumbledore's "Happy happy boom boom" speech and the Comed-Tea omnipotence gag; the Quidditch notice. | They belong to the opening of the next arc; see `LOOSE_ENDS.md`. |
| **Final image**: Harry at the Ravenclaw table under the starry ceiling, captioned as a mirror of Ep 1's garden; then Quirrell watching him. | The ending mirrors the opening (Step 10 of the method). |
